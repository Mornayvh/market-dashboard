Week in review

The week belonged to the long end. The US 10-year touched its highest level since April 2002 mid-week before settling at 5.24%, seven basis points below where it began; the five-year eased to 5.02%, the Fed funds rate held at 3.88%, and the 10s-2s spread narrowed three basis points to 0.44%. Equities absorbed the round trip unevenly. Large caps finished slightly higher, the Nasdaq 100 slipped, and the Russell 2000 fell 1.41% as smaller balance sheets wore the higher discount rate. A report that OpenAI's annualised revenue was materially below prior estimates knocked technology on Thursday, and consumer confidence reached a five-month low as pump prices followed crude higher.

Commodities did the heavy lifting. Brent rose 3.86% to 104.19, giving back ground only on Friday after Washington signalled no strikes on Iran before the midterms, and is 66.09% higher over twelve months. Copper added 2.20%, extending a 38.82% year as AI data-centre and grid demand met a decade of mine underinvestment; silver is up 30.35% and gold 5.20%. Bitcoin, by contrast, fell 2.04% and is down 27.03% on the year — the bid for hard assets has not extended to digital ones. Credit stayed unbothered throughout: high yield widened three basis points to 315, investment grade tightened two to 102, and the VIX closed at 14.84.

Closer to home, the rand firmed 0.39% to 16.59 and is 3.51% stronger against the dollar over twelve months, helped by the commodity complex. The JSE All Share added 0.55% on the week but remains 1.00% lower over the year — a conspicuous gap against the S&P's 19.21% and the Nasdaq's 27.50%. Sterling was unchanged at 1.32, the euro softened to 1.12, and the yen continued to give way at 158.43.

Week in review

Financials did the damage. Bank of America fell 13.4% over the month, Goldman Sachs 13.0% and Morgan Stanley 11.4%, with KKR down 10.0% and Apollo 8.0%. The sector has been in correction since mid-August on a more hawkish Fed and the arithmetic of repeating an exceptional second quarter in trading. Sell-side estimates have followed: Goldman's third-quarter earnings forecast has been cut by roughly a tenth over thirty days and Bank of America's by seven per cent. Results land next week. The read-across to Apollo and KKR looks more like sector beta than anything specific to either book.

Technology went the other way. Palo Alto gained 26.7% to $418.8 as enterprises funded defence against autonomous AI agents — fourth-quarter sales rose 34% and next-generation security annual recurring revenue 63%, and essentially the whole sell-side lifted targets after the print. Valuation is now the argument rather than growth, at roughly 94 times forward earnings. Meta added 11.0% and Microsoft 8.0% on the month, though both remain modest over twelve months at -1.7% and +3.3% respectively.

Elsewhere, Deere lost 4.8% on Friday alone, on a joint FTC and USDA inquiry into agricultural equipment practices opened on 7 October and a bearish USDA crop report that hit the farm equipment complex; it still trades near 34.5 times earnings against a five-year median under 19. China tech was broadly weak, CXMT down 14.8% and Unitree 11.7%. FirstRand fell 9.2% on the month, leaving it 8.7% higher over the year.
